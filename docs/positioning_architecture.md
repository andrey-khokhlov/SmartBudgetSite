# Positioning Architecture

## Purpose

This document defines how an established Professional Identity is expressed
publicly across different platforms, audiences, and future career stages without
losing coherence.

Positioning Architecture sits between Professional Narrative and all
platform-specific materials. It governs what public positioning must preserve,
what it may adapt, and how different artifacts remain parts of one Professional
Trust Architecture.

It does not define platform copy, profile structure, publication tactics, or
short-term visibility strategies. It is a durable architectural layer for
translating one professional identity into multiple appropriate public
expressions.

## Source Hierarchy

The canonical flow is:

```text
Evidence Library
        ↓
Professional Identity
        ↓
Professional Narrative
        ↓
Positioning Architecture
        ↓
Platform-specific materials
```

Each layer has a distinct responsibility:

- **Evidence Library** establishes verified facts, demonstrated capabilities,
  outcomes, contribution boundaries, and limits on public claims.
- **Professional Identity** defines the enduring mission, recurring problem,
  method, and engineering principles demonstrated across the evidence.
- **Professional Narrative** gives that identity a coherent professional story,
  connecting career evolution without changing the underlying identity.
- **Positioning Architecture** defines the invariants and adaptation rules that
  govern public expression of the narrative.
- **Platform-specific materials** select the appropriate detail, emphasis,
  format, and language for a particular context.

Authority flows downward. Lower layers may adapt language, select evidence, and
change emphasis, but they must not redefine, contradict, or silently expand
higher layers. When a lower-layer need appears to require a different identity
or an unsupported claim, the lower layer must change unless new evidence
justifies reconsidering the relevant higher layer.

## Identity Before Technology

Professional positioning must begin with professional purpose, not current
implementation tools.

It must never be built around:

- programming languages;
- BI tools;
- frameworks;
- a current employer;
- a temporary product.

Technologies demonstrate capability. Identity explains purpose.

A technology may provide strong evidence of what was built or how a problem was
solved. It cannot, by itself, explain why the work matters or what connects it to
other career stages. Tools should therefore appear as evidence and means of
implementation, not as the organizing principle of the professional identity.

This distinction allows positioning to remain coherent when technologies,
employers, products, and delivery platforms change.

## Problem Before Solution

Public positioning should communicate the professional logic in this order:

```text
Recurring problem
        ↓
Professional approach
        ↓
Relevant evidence and outcomes
        ↓
Technologies used
```

The recurring problem establishes why the work exists. The professional
approach shows how that problem is addressed. Evidence demonstrates that the
approach has been applied in reality. Technologies then explain how particular
solutions were implemented.

Positioning should not begin with tool lists. A list of tools may describe
technical exposure, but it does not communicate the decision problem being
solved, the method used, or the value of the resulting system.

## SmartBudget Positioning

SmartBudget is the current product expression of the established Professional
Identity.

It follows naturally from the evolution documented in Professional Identity:
from evidence and reliable information, through financial reporting and
Business Intelligence, toward decision-support systems that help people
understand consequences and act with confidence.

SmartBudget must not appear as:

- a hobby;
- an unrelated startup;
- an isolated side project.

It should demonstrate the integration of financial understanding, information
modelling, deterministic logic, human-centred automation, and evolvable
architecture in service of a real decision.

SmartBudget strengthens professional positioning when it is presented as a
substantial current expression of the broader professional evolution. It must
not replace that evolution or become the sole definition of the professional
identity. Future products should be able to fit the same identity when they
address the same enduring mission through different domains or technologies.

## Evidence-Based Positioning

Every public professional claim must be supportable through the Evidence
Library. Public materials may summarize evidence, but they must preserve its
meaning, contribution boundaries, and permitted scope.

Positioning must distinguish between:

- demonstrated experience;
- a reasoned interpretation of demonstrated patterns;
- current direction or aspiration;
- claims that are not sufficiently supported for public use.

Avoid unsupported expertise claims, superlatives, and inflated impact
statements. Do not convert team outcomes into individual claims, imply scale
that the evidence does not establish, or present an intended result as an
achieved result.

Credibility should emerge from specific evidence, coherent professional logic,
and appropriately bounded claims rather than from adjectives. When evidence is
insufficient, narrow or omit the claim instead of strengthening the language.

## Cross-Platform Consistency

LinkedIn, CV, GitHub, SmartBudgetSite, conference talks, publications, and
future platforms may differ in:

- detail;
- audience;
- emphasis;
- length.

They may select different evidence and use different communication styles when
their purposes genuinely differ. They must not communicate different
professional identities.

Across platforms, a reader should encounter the same underlying mission,
recurring problem, professional approach, engineering principles, and place of
SmartBudget within the broader evolution. No artifact needs to reproduce the
entire narrative, but each must remain compatible with it.

Differences of omission are acceptable when required by context. Contradictions,
unsupported expansion, and identity changes made for platform convenience are
not.

## Stability vs Adaptation

Positioning contains stable and adaptive layers.

### Stable

- mission;
- recurring problem;
- engineering principles;
- Professional Identity.

The stable layer should evolve slowly. It should change only when substantial
evidence reveals a more durable pattern, disproves the existing interpretation,
or shows that the established identity no longer explains the professional
evolution accurately.

### Adaptive

- examples;
- technologies;
- employers;
- projects;
- products;
- communication style;
- platform conventions.

The adaptive layer should evolve as professional experience grows and as the
context of communication changes. Adaptation should improve relevance and
clarity while preserving the stable layer.

The presence of change in the adaptive layer is expected. It must not trigger a
rewrite of the stable layer merely because a technology becomes fashionable, a
new employer becomes current, or a platform rewards a different style.

## Positioning Constraints

- Never optimize positioning for current technology trends.
- Never optimize positioning solely for recruiters.
- Never overfit positioning to LinkedIn or any other platform.
- Never sacrifice long-term consistency for short-term visibility.
- Never allow one employer or one product to define the whole identity.
- Never use unsupported claims to manufacture authority or impact.
- Never let platform conventions override evidence or source hierarchy.
- Always position technologies as means rather than ends.
- Always communicate the recurring problem before emphasizing its technical
  implementation.
- Always position SmartBudget within the broader professional evolution.
- Always preserve the distinction between verified evidence, interpretation,
  and aspiration.
- Always treat public launch as a separate decision from architectural
  readiness and artifact creation.

## Success Criteria

Positioning Architecture succeeds when:

- every public artifact feels like it belongs to the same professional;
- technologies may change without requiring the positioning to be rewritten;
- future products naturally fit the existing identity when they express the
  same enduring mission;
- SmartBudget strengthens rather than competes with the professional profile;
- readers can understand both what the professional does and why;
- different audiences can receive appropriate emphasis without encountering
  contradictory identities;
- every material professional claim remains traceable to evidence;
- platform-specific materials can evolve without redefining the professional
  narrative or identity.

The architecture is successful when consistency is created by shared sources
and rules, not by copying identical text across every platform.
