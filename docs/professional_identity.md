# Professional Identity

## Purpose

This document defines the enduring professional identity that gives coherence to
different roles, domains, organizations, technologies, and products. It is the
architectural source of truth for professional positioning, not a public-facing
artifact.

Professional Identity is grounded in the Evidence Library and precedes
Professional Narrative:

```text
Evidence Library
        ↓
Professional Identity
        ↓
Professional Narrative
        ↓
LinkedIn, CV, GitHub, SmartBudgetSite, presentations, and future public materials
```

The Evidence Library establishes what is true and supportable. Professional
Identity identifies the stable professional purpose, problem, method, and
principles demonstrated across that evidence. Professional Narrative then turns
that identity into a coherent account for a particular context. Platform-specific
materials adapt the narrative without redefining the identity.

This document is not a CV, LinkedIn profile, personal statement, biography, or
marketing document. It does not optimize for a platform or audience, list career
events, or attempt to persuade through unsupported claims.

## Core Mission

Help people make better decisions by transforming fragmented, evolving
operational data into reliable information they can confidently act upon.

This mission is independent of any employer, technology, programming language,
BI tool, product, or public platform. The implementation may change; the purpose
does not.

The work is therefore not defined primarily by producing reports, automating
spreadsheets, writing software, or building dashboards. Those are possible means.
The enduring objective is to create dependable decision support from operational
reality that is otherwise difficult to understand, maintain, or use with
confidence.

## Fundamental Problem

The recurring professional problem is the gap between operational data and a
decision a person can responsibly make.

Organizations and individuals often have information, but not yet a trustworthy
basis for action. The information may be:

- fragmented across people, files, processes, and systems;
- inconsistent in structure, meaning, or ownership;
- incomplete or changing while decisions still need to be made;
- dependent on repetitive manual processing;
- difficult to validate, explain, or maintain;
- embedded in systems that do not scale with new requirements;
- presented without a clear connection to the decision it should support.

In that condition, more data does not automatically create more confidence.
People may spend excessive effort reconciling inputs, repeat avoidable work, rely
on assumptions that cannot be inspected, or make decisions without sufficiently
trustworthy evidence.

This problem provides a common frame for work across EY, Stage, Dodo, OTEKO,
BLACK WALL GROUP, ArtLight, and SmartBudget. The organizational settings and
immediate outputs differ, but the durable concern remains the same: turn an
imperfect and evolving information environment into a reliable, understandable,
and usable basis for decisions.

## Method

The recurring professional approach is:

1. Understand the real decision.
   Begin with the action, judgment, or consequence that the work must support,
   rather than with a preferred tool or predetermined output.
2. Identify the required evidence.
   Determine what must be known, what is already supported, what remains
   uncertain, and how important facts can be validated.
3. Design an appropriate information model.
   Give the relevant concepts, relationships, calculations, and ownership clear
   structure so that the result reflects the problem rather than the limitations
   of the current source format.
4. Automate repetitive processing.
   Reduce manual work where rules can be made explicit and repeatable, while
   preserving appropriate human judgment and control.
5. Present trustworthy decision support.
   Make the result understandable, traceable, and connected to the decision a
   person needs to make.
6. Preserve future evolvability.
   Design for changes in data, requirements, processes, scale, and implementation
   technology without making the present solution unnecessarily complex.

This method combines analysis, financial and operational understanding,
information modelling, automation, and engineering. None of those disciplines
alone defines the identity; their integration in service of better decisions
does.

## Engineering Principles

### Evidence before assumptions

Claims, models, and decisions should begin with the strongest available evidence.
Assumptions should be explicit, bounded, and open to revision when better
evidence appears.

### Trustworthy information before decisions

Decision support is useful only when people can understand what the information
means, where it came from, and when it may no longer be reliable.

### Automation over repetitive manual work

Stable, repeatable processing should be automated when doing so reduces error,
effort, and cognitive load. Automation should release people for judgment, not
hide important reasoning from them.

### Evolvable architecture

Solutions should accommodate foreseeable change in data, rules, scope, and
delivery without requiring constant reinvention. Evolvability is a practical
quality, not an invitation to build speculative abstractions.

### Transparency over unnecessary complexity

Models, calculations, workflows, and system boundaries should remain as clear as
the problem permits. Complexity must earn its place by improving reliability,
decision value, or maintainability.

### Deterministic systems before AI interpretation

Calculations, validations, balances, plans, actuals, deviations, and timelines
belong in deterministic systems. AI may help interpret grounded results and make
them easier to understand; it should not invent the underlying facts or business
logic.

### Human-centred design

The quality of a system includes its effect on the people who use and maintain
it. Good solutions reduce avoidable manual and emotional load, preserve human
agency, and communicate uncertainty honestly.

### Architecture that survives change

Employers, platforms, technologies, teams, and products will change. Durable
concepts, explicit boundaries, and replaceable implementation choices allow the
system's decision value to survive those changes.

## Domain Evolution

The professional domains evolved over time:

```text
Audit
  ↓
Financial reporting
  ↓
Business Intelligence
  ↓
Decision-support systems
  ↓
SmartBudget
```

This is not a sequence of unrelated reinventions. Each domain expands the same
underlying concern.

Audit emphasizes evidence, consistency, and justified confidence. Financial
reporting structures operational reality into information that can be reviewed
and used. Business Intelligence makes that information more systematic,
repeatable, and accessible. Decision-support systems connect information more
directly to choices, consequences, and action. SmartBudget applies the same logic
to personal financial planning: a person needs a reliable view of future
consequences before making a decision and must be able to revise the plan when
reality changes.

The domains, users, tools, and scale changed. The professional identity remained
consistent: build trustworthy bridges between fragmented operational reality and
human decisions.

## SmartBudget

SmartBudget is a natural consequence of this professional evolution.

It brings together the recurring elements of the identity:

- a real decision that matters to a person;
- fragmented and evolving financial information;
- a need for explicit models and deterministic calculations;
- repetitive processing that benefits from careful automation;
- results that must be understandable before they can be trusted;
- an architecture that must evolve without obscuring the user's control.

SmartBudget extends the progression from validating information, to structuring
it, to making it operational, and finally to building a product around the
decision itself. Its forecasting-first approach reflects the same enduring
mission: help people act with greater confidence by making consequences visible
before a decision is made and by showing when new evidence requires a plan to
change.

For that reason, SmartBudget should not be positioned as a hobby, a detached
startup experiment, or an incidental side project. It is the current product
expression of a longer professional pattern. At the same time, the professional
identity is broader and more durable than SmartBudget; no single product should
become its sole foundation.

## Long-Term Stability

Technologies, employers, programming languages, BI tools, products, markets, and
public platforms may change. Professional Identity should remain substantially
stable because it describes the enduring mission, recurring problem, working
method, and engineering principles rather than their current implementation.

Future evidence may refine this document when it reveals a genuinely durable
pattern or disproves an existing interpretation. It should not be rewritten to
match short-term platform conventions, recruitment trends, or a new technology.

Professional Narrative, LinkedIn, CV, GitHub, SmartBudgetSite, presentations,
portfolio materials, and future public artifacts should be derived from this
document and checked against the Evidence Library. They may select different
details and language for different purposes, but they should not create a
different professional identity.
